Act One
Zone trading & why parameters matter
1

What is grid trading?

Grid trading divides a price range into zones and places orders at each level. When price dips to a lower level, it buys; when price rises to the next level, it sells. It harvests profit from the price oscillating up and down — without ever predicting which way the market will go.

Because it only reacts to price touching a level, it works especially well in choppy, sideways markets where prices swing but don't trend strongly.

PRINCIPLE1
Cashflow, not direction
Grid trading never bets on the market going up or down. It collects cashflow from volatility itself — the goal is a stream of cash, not a correct forecast.
2

Parameters are everything

The outcome of a grid depends entirely on how it is configured. Three groups of parameters shape the result:

Range / trigger
the upper & lower bounds — where trading fires versus pauses
Frequency
how many zones and how far apart — how often trades fire
Capital distribution
how much money is committed to each zone

Change any one of them slightly and the cashflow can look completely different — more trades, fewer trades, bigger harvest, or capital left idle.

PRINCIPLE2
Same market, different outcome
On the exact same price path, different parameters produce completely different cashflow. The entire challenge is setting them right.
3

The catch — the best setting keeps changing

The parameters that are ideal in a calm market are wrong the moment the market turns volatile — and the market regime shifts constantly. There is no single configuration you can set once and leave alone.

This is what makes it a genuine decision problem: the right answer depends on the conditions at that moment, and those conditions keep moving.

PRINCIPLE3
No fixed optimum
No single set of parameters is best forever. The optimum depends on the current market — so the setting must adapt to context.

Act Two
What our model does
4

The big idea

Instead of a person guessing and hand-tuning the grid, we build a model that chooses the parameters automatically — aiming for the best possible cashflow given the current market.

PRINCIPLE4
Learn the setting, not the forecast
We are not building a model to predict prices. We are building a model that chooses the parameters that lead to the best outcome.
5

What does "best" mean? — the objective

Recall two earlier principles: we care about cashflow, not direction (Principle 1), and there is no fixed optimum (Principle 3). Together they tell us what to measure — the quality of the cash stream itself.

So "best" has to be something we can measure. We define an objective function with two goals: we want cashflow that is high, and cashflow that is consistent — steady period after period, not a big harvest one month and nothing the next.

Together these form a single score we call cashflow quality: high average, low swing.

In plain terms, the model is rewarded for: high & steady cashflow per period
PRINCIPLE5
Maximise cashflow, minimise its swing
The goal isn't just the largest cashflow — it's cashflow that is high and consistent. Investors want income they can rely on.
6

The causal rule

This is the heart of the design. The model looks only at market data that occurred before price enters a zone, and then decides the parameters. It never peeks at the future.

This matters for two reasons:

  • Honest results — using only past information prevents look-ahead bias, so the measured performance isn't fooling itself.
  • Deployable for real — in live trading you only ever have the past, so training this way means it works in practice, not just on paper.
PRINCIPLE6
Decide with the past only (causal)
The model sets parameters using data from before price enters the zone. Results don't cheat, and the method transfers directly to live trading.

How it works
7

The pipeline

Everything connects in one loop: market context feeds the model, the model picks parameters, we simulate the trading, and we measure the cashflow it produced. That measurement is used to improve the model.

Market context
recent price behaviour
→
Model
picks parameters
→
Simulate trading
run the grid
→
Cashflow quality
score the outcome

The score flows back to improve the model — repeated until it gets good.

PRINCIPLE7
One end-to-end loop
Market data → choose parameters → simulate trading → measure cashflow → improve the model. Repeat until it's good.
8

Why this is "decision-focused"

The model is trained on the cashflow it actually produces — the outcome of its decision — rather than on how accurately it predicts prices. That is exactly what "Decision-Focused Learning" means: the training target is the real goal.

The usual approach would predict the market first and then optimise separately — but a good forecast doesn't guarantee good cashflow. By training on the decision's outcome directly, the model optimises what we truly care about, not a proxy for it.

PRINCIPLE8
Train on the outcome we care about
We don't reward the model for predicting prices accurately. We reward it for decisions that produce good cashflow — the training goal equals the real goal.

Act Four
The hard part
9

Why it's technically hard

Here is the real obstacle. The trading simulation is made of discrete actions — a trade fires only when price touches a level, otherwise nothing happens. This makes it non-differentiable.

Training a neural network normally relies on sending a gradient — a smooth "which way to adjust" signal — backwards through every step. But you can't pass that signal cleanly through an on/off trading rule.

PRINCIPLE9
The decision layer isn't differentiable
Trading is on/off at each trigger, so a gradient can't flow straight through it. This is the central ML challenge of the project.
10

How we get around it

When we can't send the gradient straight through, we estimate it instead. The model tries different parameter choices, sees which produced better cashflow, and nudges itself in that direction — a technique known as a score-function estimator. An alternative is to build a smooth surrogate of the simulation that is differentiable.

Either way, the model can still be trained end-to-end toward better cashflow. Making this non-differentiable decision problem trainable is the core contribution — it's what lets a cashflow objective drive parameter selection directly, which existing grid methods can't do.

PRINCIPLE10
Estimate the gradient instead
When back-propagation can't pass through, we approximate the direction by sampling choices and comparing outcomes — keeping the whole system trainable end-to-end.

Looking ahead
Hierarchical extension
→

Zooming in: sub-zones

A natural next step is to look inside each zone. Instead of one flat grid, we split each big zone into sub-zones, give each big zone its own fixed budget, and let a single shared model decide the sub-structure whenever price enters.

This gives two practical advantages: a much denser training signal (every zone visit becomes a training example), and an event-driven, resettable rule that mirrors exactly how you'd trade it live.

Why zoom in?

Every time price enters a zone becomes a fresh decision to learn from — so the model sees far more examples — and because one shared model applies to every zone, it deploys cleanly in real trading.

The idea, in one line

A recap of the whole story — from what grid trading is, to what makes our approach different.

The goal
Harvest a high, steady stream of cashflow from price swings — not a bet on market direction.
The problem
The right parameters keep changing with the market, so they can't be set once by hand.
Our model
Chooses those parameters automatically, using only past data, trained directly on cashflow quality.
The challenge
The trading simulation isn't differentiable — making it trainable is the core contribution.
In one line: a model that learns the grid settings which produce the best, most consistent cashflow — trained on the decision's real outcome, not on price predictions.
PRINCIPLE12
De-risk before we build
The riskiest part — proving the gradient can flow through the trading simulation — is tackled first. If it holds, the rest follows; if not, we adjust the direction early, before investing full effort.
